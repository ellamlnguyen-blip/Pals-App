# ADR-0018 — Disposable-local blocking and reporting

Status: Accepted — disposable-local TASK-016 stages only
Date: 2026-09-23
Task: TASK-016

## Context
Accepted ADR-0013/0014/0016 implement bilateral People suppression, friendship teardown and DM termination using a directional private block relation. ADR-0010/0015 explicitly defer Hangout/private-location/chat precedence. MVP requires reporting users and Hangouts; USER_FLOWS requires reporting after removal. No accepted policy yet defines those extensions, report evidence or report retention. TASK-010/ADR-0012 remains independently blocked.

## Decision
Acceptance authorizes only narrower reviewed, main-published disposable-local stages. No hosted migration, deployment, live-student operation, Realtime, production retention or moderation authority follows. Existing feature gates stay default off. This ADR supersedes only the local People-only/Hangout block exceptions in ADR-0010/0013/0015 once implemented; other accepted privacy boundaries remain.

### One directional block relation
Reuse the existing private block relation as the single authority; do not create competing People and global lists. Either direction suppresses People discovery, friendship formation and DM access. Blocking atomically tears down friendship and terminates active DM generations even if their delivery gates are off. Unblock removes only the caller's direction and never restores a relationship, conversation or membership automatically. Existing DM directional suppression remains intact.

Introduce a separate default-off safety gate controlling new block-management/report operations. Existing block RPC entry points must delegate to the same policy and cannot retain an old weaker write path. Stored blocks remain enforced by every enabled source feature even when safety or People management gates are off. Disabling a safety UI must never disable existing separation. Active owners may list at most 24 outbound IDs per keyset page and unblock by exact full ID with the safety gate on even after readiness loss. No incoming-block list, names, timestamps, labels or peer photo expansion; suspended/banned accounts cannot manage blocks.

A new block requires an active caller and either current authorized peer visibility or retained caller-specific evidence: an existing owned block, current or retained friendship/DM participation, or a Hangout participation relationship with that target established before revocation. Evidence must exist in authoritative records, never a supplied ID or prior browser state alone. Historical evidence allows an active caller to block after their own readiness/campus/opt-in loss without reopening peer data. A Hangout relationship means the target is its immutable host or a proven overlapping participant; arbitrary current campus membership is not enough. Unknown/unauthorized targets share a neutral unavailable response. A desired-state retry changes nothing twice.

For pre-migration history, two participant rows sharing a Hangout ID alone never prove overlap. Accept only overlap provable from retained authoritative intervals; otherwise fail closed for that peer target. Do not reconstruct discarded history or invent historical friendship evidence from a retry key that lacks the peer. From this migration forward, preserve minimal private immutable caller/peer/Hangout overlap provenance before leave/remove/rejoin can overwrite the current interval, including block reconciliation. Retained DM generations and the immutable host of a Hangout with the caller’s own membership remain independently sufficient evidence. A safety-gated active caller may recover only their own retained Hangout IDs and own membership state through a keyset projection of at most 24 rows, including after readiness loss, with no title, host/peer IDs, time, location or target details. No peer history list is added.

### Hangout separation
The goal is no blocked pair sharing current authorized attendance and private coordination, without letting an attendee evict someone else from another person's Hangout.

| Situation at a new block | Committed effect |
| --- | --- |
| Blocker is the Hangout host and target is joined | Target becomes removed (terminal under ADR-0010). Host remains joined. |
| Blocker is a nonhost and both are joined | Blocker leaves that Hangout; target is not evicted. This also applies when target is host. |
| No shared joined Hangout | No membership change. |

These effects apply to all retained shared joined Hangouts, including cancelled records; cancellation still denies private/chat access to everyone. Host ownership never transfers. A block cannot cancel another person's Hangout. Explain departure/removal before confirmation. Store minimal private provenance for these safety transitions; emit no block, forced-leave or forced-removal notifications that disclose the relationship. No report is automatically submitted.

Future joins require no either-direction block with any current joined participant, including a currently unready participant. Otherwise return generic unavailable without identifying the conflicting member. Joined hosts cannot be evicted through another student's block. A voluntarily departed blocker can deliberately rejoin after the conflict is gone if ordinary eligibility/joining rules allow; a removed participant cannot rejoin. Unblocking alone does not restore attendance.

A viewer with a block in either direction against the host cannot discover/read that Hangout through map, calendar, list, detail, direct table/embeds, roster, private instructions or chat. A third-party host's public Hangout may remain discoverable when only an attendee is blocked; roster projections suppress blocked account IDs in both directions and do not expose a blocked-member count/reason. Joining remains denied while a conflicting member is joined. The caller can always obtain their own minimal participation state for cleanup/reporting without receiving hidden target data. Host management may return only the minimal nonpublic state already authorized under ADR-0010; no peer profile bypass.

Leaving/removal immediately revokes new private-instruction and group-chat reads/sends. Additionally omit historical messages authored by a blocked peer from every future group-chat projection, including after the author left; suppress the whole message, not just its author ID. Pagination filters before limiting and does not use a hidden message as proof of access. Remaining eligible members retain the thread under ADR-0015. The app cannot retract information already delivered, prevent off-app contact or guarantee physical separation.

Existing stored People blocks must be reconciled atomically before Hangout access can reopen after the new migration: for each conflicting joined pair, remove the nonhost target of a host-owned block; otherwise mark every nonhost blocker in a conflicting pair left. Compute the affected set from the initial snapshot, with host-removal taking precedence, so cycles/mutual blocks do not depend on row order. Retain pre-transition evidence. Gates remain off through reconciliation. This is a disposable-local migration, not authorization to apply these transitions to hosted users.

### Notifications and concurrent enforcement
All existing and future inbox projections reauthorize under the new source rules. A revoked item yields only its neutral unavailable representation; no actor, target/link, title, message or block reason leaks. Historical group-chat notification items whose message author is blocked must also become neutral even if the recipient still has thread access. Block-triggered membership changes produce no ordinary leave/host-activity notification. Existing nonconflicting events preserve ADR-0017 preferences and cancellation behavior.

All authorization remains in database policies/caller-bound RPCs, including direct REST, embeds, old RPCs and server actions. Client-controlled actor/role/campus claims are never authority. READ COMMITTED is required for new safety operations; stronger isolation fails closed. Blocking, join/leave/remove/cancel, friendship/DM mutations and message writes must share a documented deadlock-safe lock order and freshly recheck authorization after contention. New membership cannot slip into a Hangout between the block's affected-set query and commit. Stage A must prove this across multiple shared Hangouts, opposite blocks and concurrent joins, with observed lock waits, before any dependent stage is dispatched. Source gates being off must not prevent authorized safety teardown. A source mutation committed before a block may stand; subsequent reads must apply the committed block. An already-authorized in-flight read may finish, consistent with ADR-0015; no retroactive recall claim.

### Reports and access after removal
Reports target a user or Hangout only. Message-target reports, attachments, copied message/profile/location snapshots and automatic sanctions are excluded. A report is an allegation, not an established finding. The submitted target, category and narrative must never be shown to the reported user or ordinary peers.

An active caller can report a currently authorized user/Hangout, or use retained caller-specific evidence after blocking, leaving, removal, cancellation, opt-out, campus change or readiness loss. For a Hangout, their own retained membership in any state is sufficient. For a user, require the same historical relationship evidence defined for blocking, including the immutable host of a previously joined Hangout. Reports do not require People opt-in or source feature gates when historical evidence suffices; they do require the safety gate and active account. Suspended/banned callers have no client reporting route in this local increment; appeals need separate policy. A removed student can report the Hangout/host using opaque IDs without regaining public details, roster, instructions or chat. A report-host action may supply the caller’s retained Hangout ID as provenance: the server resolves its immutable host into the private user report and returns only the opaque receipt, never a host lookup result. This avoids requiring the removed caller to retain the host ID. No arbitrary-ID target lookup, searchable report-target directory or raw evidence reader is introduced.

Use a private report table and private caller-scoped retry ledger with RLS and no client SELECT/DML grants. Store server ID/time, reporter, target type/ID, category, trimmed optional plain-text narrative (maximum 2000 characters), and minimal server-derived provenance reference. Categories: harassment, safety concern, impersonation, spam/commercial promotion, other. Require 1–2000 characters for other; otherwise narrative may be empty. No uploads or URLs treated as executable content. Preserve existing source evidence privately; do not make copies of entire conversations or exact locations. New minimal provenance may retain participant IDs and Hangout IDs privately to support post-removal reports; never expose an attendance history to peers.

A caller-scoped UUID and normalized payload fingerprint deduplicate retries. Same key/same payload returns only that caller's original opaque receipt and server submission time after checking safety gate and active account; it does not reveal current target existence or reopen target data. Different payload with the same key fails generically. A deliberate new report uses a new key. Apply an atomic maximum of five new reports per caller per rolling hour, independent of target; exact retries do not consume the limit. Denial and throttling must not reveal hidden target existence. No report list, narrative readback, edit/delete, case status, moderator reader, service-role web proxy or automatic punishment is included. UI confirms only that the report was saved locally, with no claim of staffed review or emergency response.

Reports, retry/provenance and existing retained source records persist only for disposable-local verification and are cleared in cleanup. Production retention/deletion, evidence access, audited review, appeals and operational response belong to TASK-017 or a separately accepted prerequisite before hosted operation. Platform roles grant no new reader here.

## Consequences and alternatives
Strict separation may cause the blocker to leave several shared Hangouts and deny a join without naming the conflict. This is preferable for this proposal to granting arbitrary attendee removal or allowing continued shared private coordination. A softer mute-only group policy was considered but would leave exact meeting details and future chat available to blocked peers. Existing ID-only outbound block management remains a documented local usability limitation; identifying blocked users with retained names/photos requires a later privacy decision.

## Acceptance evidence
After the independently reviewed proposal was published and canonical main verified at `3d4fec11d13c1f936bb89e7012e20cae7f4151bd`, the coordinator asked, “Do you accept ADR-0018 as written for local-only implementation?” The user explicitly replied “yes” on 2026-09-23. This accepts the complete local blocking, Hangout separation, provenance, reporting and retention policy above. Each narrower stage still requires reviewed canonical publication before dispatch. No hosted operation, production retention, moderation access or deployment is authorized. TASK-010/ADR-0012 remains Proposed and independent.
